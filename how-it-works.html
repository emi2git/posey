How it works

How Posey sees your form

A small AI model finds your joints, a bit of geometry measures the angles, and simple coaching rules decide what to say. All of it runs in your browser.

Data flow

Everything here runs on your device
  1. CameraVideo frames, about 30 a second
  2. Pose modelFinds 33 body points per frame
  3. Angle mathKnee angle, back lean, arm height
  4. Exercise rulesDeep enough? Arms high enough?
  5. FeedbackRep count, cues and voice

1. The camera

When you press Start camera, your browser asks for permission. The video goes to a canvas on the page and nowhere else.

2. The pose model

Posey uses MediaPipe Pose Landmarker, a small open model from Google (about 5 MB). For every frame it finds 33 points on your body: shoulders, elbows, hips, knees, ankles and more. It runs on your device's graphics chip when it can, or the processor when it can't.

3. Angles, not guesswork

The form check itself is plain geometry. For a squat, Posey measures the angle at your knee (hip, knee, ankle) and how far your back leans from vertical. For jumping jacks, it compares wrist height to your head and foot spread to your shoulders.

4. Exercise rules

Each exercise has its own rules. A rep counts only when it is complete, so half reps don't pad your number. Instead, Posey tells you what to fix.

5. The voice coach

  • Every clean rep is counted out loud: "One", "Two", "Three"…
  • Corrections such as "Go lower" or "Chest up" are spoken straight away and take priority over the count.
  • A little encouragement every fifth rep, never the same phrase twice in a row.
  • Pick any voice your device offers, or mute the coach with one tap.

Tips for a good read

  • Keep your whole body in frame, head to feet.
  • Use good, even light and a plain background if you can.
  • Prop the device up so it stays still, about 2–3 metres away.
  • Follow the setup for each exercise: side-on for squats, facing the camera for jumping jacks.
